# Changes

## v2

**Not released.** v2 is a possible future edition, kept here in `v2/` and not linked from the site. v1, the minted collection, is kept unchanged in [`v1/`](../v1/). Its known issues are in [`v1/NOTES.md`](../v1/NOTES.md); v2 fixes all eight. The player at the site root already has the viewer fixes (1, 2, 4 and 8), the spawn fix (7) and the mobile game, with v1's art.

**Same seed, different art.** Changes 5 and 6 change how a seed is turned into traits, so many seeds produce different art in v2 than their v1 token. To see a seed as minted, open it in the player at the site root (`makimono.psr.fyi/?seed=<hex>`).

### Fixes

1. **Edge bleed on scrolling patterns.** The scrolling filling layer is wrapped in a fixed clip, so no sliver of filling shows past the art's right edge in the detailed view. ([v1 issue 1](../v1/NOTES.md#1-edge-bleed-on-scrolling-patterns-in-the-detailed-view))
2. **Mobile tap opens the traits band.** Taps are detected from pointer/touch events instead of waiting for a `click` that iOS Safari never sends. Timing uses `e.timeStamp`, since Aleatory freezes `Date.now()`. ([v1 issue 2](../v1/NOTES.md#2-tapping-the-art-doesnt-open-the-traits-band-on-mobile))
3. **Katamaki plays on phones and tablets.** Instead of hiding the KATAMAKI button on mobile, the game now has touch controls (see below). ([v1 issue 3](../v1/NOTES.md#3-katamaki-button-shows-on-mobile-where-the-game-isnt-supported))
4. **GIF export follows the current art.** The cached GIF is only reused while the art (including the Message) is the same as when it was rendered. Every GIF shows the hopped version, whatever the hop toggle. ([v1 issue 4](../v1/NOTES.md#4-gif-export-keeps-an-earlier-version-after-the-message-changes))
7. **Katamaki starts with a clear spot.** Nothing starts stuck to the roll: anything placed near the start is moved just outside a small clear circle, onto land. The rest of the world is unchanged. ([v1 issue 7](../v1/NOTES.md#7-katamaki-can-start-with-pieces-already-stuck-to-the-roll))
8. **No seams on iPhone Safari.** The art is drawn as flat full frames: each distinct frame is one complete picture on whole pixels, with no sliding layers or clip, shown for exactly the 70ms slots the layered art shows it. The animation reads the same, slot for slot, and iPhone Safari no longer shows hairlines around the filling or along the edge. Hop off is a second flat version (rest pose, fills keep running). ([v1 issue 8](../v1/NOTES.md#8-hairline-seams-around-the-filling-and-the-arts-edge-on-iphone-safari))

### Rule changes

5. **Mono follows the Profile.** Mono colours now follow the Profile using the same 4 shades: Random uses at least 3 shades, Trio `a a b b c c`, Mirror `a b c c b a`, Duo and Accent use two shades at least 2 steps apart, and Spectrum keeps v1's look. Mono also gets the same no-Fast speed limits as Classic and Neon. ([v1 issue 5](../v1/NOTES.md#5-mono-ignores-the-profile-trait))
6. **No lookalike combinations.** Combinations that showed the same art as another are no longer picked. ([v1 issue 6](../v1/NOTES.md#6-some-trait-combinations-look-the-same-as-others))
   - All palettes: Duo can't get Diagonal or Chase; Accent can't get Checker.
   - Mono: Accent, Mirror and Random can't get Text; Mirror can't get Sparkle, Ripple or Maki.

The listed odds still add up: pattern odds come from each profile's allowed patterns, and the speed shares are recomputed from them.

### Katamaki on mobile

- **Touch controls.** Drag anywhere for an analog stick that appears under your thumb: drag the way you want to roll (see Driving, below). An arrow button hops. A round button under the timer pauses (44px, styled like the hop button); the pause screen has Resume, Restart and **Back to art**, so leaving the game is never one stray tap away. The touch hint fades after 8 seconds of play, like the keyboard one. The page doesn't scroll or zoom while playing.
- **Driving.** The roll handles like a car's front wheel, and the camera follows it from a fixed angle, high and 40° down, without swinging round behind it, so up the screen always means the same way. Drag the way you want to go: the roll steers toward it and curves round, turning faster the faster it rolls (it can still pivot slowly when stopped). Drag well behind it to back up that way. It takes about a second to reach full speed and coasts to a stop over about a second when you let go, turning is a quarter heavier at full speed, and hitting something too big stops it. Pushing up after a turn no longer straightens the roll. On the keyboard, W/S are the throttle and A/D steer, with the same momentum.
- **Portrait and landscape.** The HUD keeps clear of the notch and home bar, the controls sit above the size bar in portrait, and portrait gets a wider camera view.
- **Touch icons.** The jump, pause, zoom and camera buttons use [Phosphor](https://phosphoricons.com)'s bold icons, filled in solid (MIT licence): an arrow over a bar for jump, two bars for pause, a magnifier with + that turns to − while zoomed in, and a camera with a ring lens.
- **Pauses when hidden.** Switching apps or locking the phone pauses the round.
- **Adaptive quality.** Quality has four levels, Ultra, High, Medium and Low, and under 48 fps it steps down one: High caps sharpness at 1.5×, Medium goes to 1× with fewer decor cones and clouds and no antialiasing from the next round, and Low goes to 0.75× with no scattered scenery, and pieces on the far side of the roll, which the roll hides anyway, aren't drawn (they show again for a photo, when knocked off and for the ending), so a big roll late in a round doesn't slow an old PC down. Collectibles, ants and chopsticks never change. A step that doesn't help is undone, but only at 30 fps or more; below 20 fps it goes straight to Low. A few long stalls (switching apps, for example) spoil a measurement, but when most frames are slow the device is just slow, so it still steps down: before, a very slow start could leave quality stuck high, with hops too quick to see. `?perf=1` shows the frame rate and level (tap it to step Low, Medium, High, Ultra, then back to auto); `?quality=low`, `medium`, `high` or `ultra` forces a level. The older `?tier=0`–`3` still works (0 is Ultra, 3 Low).
- **Mobile viewport.** The page now has a mobile viewport tag, so phones lay it out at their real width instead of a shrunk desktop page.

### Other

- The project name in the top-left corner of the traits view reads **MAKIMONO 2.0**.
- Katamaki's welcome screen shows a small "Welcome to" line above **KATAMAKI**, built from the same 3D cube letters as the message in the sky. The letters take the piece's colours, in the same order a custom message on that piece would, and bob and turn like the sky letters. Under the title, your own maki turns slowly, with the same filling layout you play with. Both stay still if the device asks for reduced motion.
- **Text ANTS** pieces get a real ant planet in Katamaki: 30 ants instead of 15. The usual 9 are joined by 21 small ants you can roll up from the start. They never knock you back, and each adds a little size (0.05, versus 0.16 to 0.23 for a normal ant). The same applies to any message containing "ants". Other pieces keep 9 ants.
- **Chase** pieces get a magnet in Katamaki instead of pulling every pickup in at the start. Pickups and ants you can already roll up are drawn in from within a range around the roll (about 6.5 units at the start, growing as the roll grows), faster the closer they get. Anything still too big stays put until you've grown, so the magnet reaches further as you get bigger. To balance it, Chase pieces have 9 chopsticks instead of 3.
- The two wave patterns are renamed for what they are: **Wiggle** is now **Wave**, and **Turbo Wiggle** (the same wave at half the wavelength) is now **Short Wave**. Only the names change.

- Katamaki rebuilds its world when the seed changes, like the player. On chain the seed never changes, so nothing differs there.
- The page around the art takes the piece's background colour, edge to edge, with or without the traits shown, and so does the browser bar on iPhone Safari and Android Chrome (`theme-color`). Before, the art sat on a dark grey page. The player has this too. With the traits hidden, the art sits at the bottom of the screen in portrait; with them shown it's centred.

### Keys and the end screen

- **One meaning per key.** `P` only pauses and resumes. `Enter` is the go key: start, keep rolling, confirm restart. `R` restarts. `Esc` cancels the restart question, otherwise goes back to the art. The buttons show these keys as keycaps (hidden on touch, and so are the key caps in the More menu). In v1, `P` also meant Continue rolling and Cancel, and on the end screen it did nothing: Keep rolling is now on every end screen.
- **New art keys:** `E` saves the SVG, `Shift`+`E` the GIF. `?` shows a card with every shortcut, in the piece's colours; a click on it or anywhere else closes it. The More menu also opens it, from its last item, Keyboard Shortcuts (not shown on touch). The menu is as wide as its longest label, so no label wraps.
- **Game hints** show the keys as keycaps in groups, at the bottom (roll, steer, hop, pause, restart) and at the top (`G` / `Esc` art). Both fade out after 8 seconds of play and come back on any pause or end screen. Touch keeps its text hint.
- **Size and goal** show as a scoreboard row over the bar, in the same style as OBJECTS and the timer. The timer no longer adds a ★ once the goal is reached.
- **Size bar with medal marks.** The bar now starts empty at your starting size (1×) and is full at gold, so it runs past the goal instead of stopping there. Thin soft grey marks show where bronze (the goal) and silver are. The bar stays white. The GOAL number shows the next medal to reach: the goal, then silver, then gold, and stays on gold once you have it. The pause screen shows the same next medal (Size 4.2× / Gold 4.5×), and the end screen with a medal says how far it was to the next one ("0.3× short of gold", or "past gold"). The start screen and the time's-up lines still give the round's goal, the minimum to win. Sizes are always rounded down to 0.1 (3.17× reads 3.1×), so a size never shows a goal or medal it hasn't reached: losing at 3.19× of 3.2× reads 3.1×.
- **New end-screen lines.** Gold, silver and bronze each have 4 or 5 titles, time's up has 6, and ants show up in at most one line per screen. Sizes read `1.2×` everywhere, like the scoreboard.
- **A useful hop.** While you're in the air, low things (small rocks, bushes, ingredients too big to roll up) don't block you. Chopsticks and ants can still hit you. The hop grows with the roll, so at gold size it's about 4× higher and lasts about 0.75 s (0.42 s at the start). The shadow shrinks while you're up, so you can judge the height. There's no cooldown. Before, the hop was only a fixed bounce, and a wall still stopped you mid-air.
- **Clouds can be rolled up.** Once the top of the roll reaches a cloud, it sticks like anything else and adds a little size. Over the sea that's around the goal size with a hop; from high ground, sooner.
- **Edges.** Things too big to roll up stop you closer to them: scenery stops you at the same reach as oversized food (0.85 of the roll plus 0.7 of the thing, instead of the whole roll plus the whole thing). And brushing past one no longer stops you dead: the roll slides round its edge, a little slowed. Heading straight into it, or sliding into something else, still stops you.
- **Secret `T`:** throws away everything the roll has collected, all at once, the same throw a chopstick hit uses, and the roll goes back to its starting size. The pieces land around you and can be rolled up again, each worth its share of what the roll had grown (before, rolling them back up could push the size to a huge number). Not listed in the shortcuts card.
- **Every ant is named.** Rolling up an ant that walks the planet now shows GOT ANT and counts in the ant total, like anthill ants. Before, only anthill and belt ants did.
- Opening the game drops keyboard focus from the Message cell, so the game's keys always work.

## The preview page: B-SIDE

`v2/index.html` is no longer a preview of the edition above. It's the player (minted art, exactly as on the token) plus **B-SIDE**: an optional alternate take on each minted token, drawn with gentler rules than the v2 edition. The minted token itself never changes, and nothing on Aleatory changes. It also has v2's Katamaki and the changes below. Since the B-SIDE release it's also the root player (`index.html`, makimono.psr.fyi); this copy is for trying changes first.

### B-SIDE rules

A branch of the v2 edition's rules that keeps each token recognisable:

- **Kept exactly as minted:** pattern, message and speed (v1's speed rules and shares), and every other random choice: profile, direction, tone, nori, rice, shape, roll and the details. The main random stream advances exactly as in v1. The lookalike rules (6) are off; they only matter when picking new seeds.
- **Changed:** Mono colours follow the Profile, as in rule 5 (Trio `a a b b c c`, Mirror `a b c c b a`, Duo and Accent two shades 2+ steps apart, Random 3+ shades, Spectrum unchanged). The shade picks come from their own stream (`seed + "|alt"`), so nothing else moves. Rice can turn white if a new shade is too close to it (v1's contrast rule).
- **Offered only when it's visible:** the biggest colour change of any pixel, over the whole loop, is at least 30 ΔE, **and** either it brings new shades (some colour is 10+ ΔE from every colour in the other version) or it moves the same shades around a lot (2%+ of all pixels, over the loop, change by 30+ ΔE). A few swapped pixels alone don't count. Otherwise the B-SIDE button doesn't show and the piece shows its minted art. Tuned on the minted tokens in the trait finder.

The traits use the new wave names (Wave, Short Wave) on both sides.

### The page

- **Buttons:** KATAMAKI · B-SIDE · ⋯. B-SIDE is a toggle (it stays pressed while on) and adds `&art=b` to the address, so a shared link opens on the B-side. A new seed starts on its minted art. ⋯ opens a menu: Download SVG, Download GIF, View Seed (opens the seed field) and Random Seed, each with its key.
- **Keyboard shortcuts:** the v2 edition's keys (above), plus `B` B-side, `V` view seed and `N` new seed. The shortcuts card lists them all.
- **Phones and small windows** (touch screens, or a window whose shorter side is under 600 px): with the traits shown, the buttons move from the top right of the poster to a bar just below the traits, as wide as them. Labels are the trait values' size. KATAMAKI fills the bar (with B-SIDE beside it); with a wallet list, the wallet name fills it instead. The ⋯ menu and the wallet list open upward. Larger windows keep the buttons inside the poster.
- **Katamaki** starts from the v2 edition's game (keys, hints, scoreboard and end-screen lines above) and adds the changes in **Katamaki on this page**, below. The v2 edition (`makimono.html`, the mint build) keeps the game as described above.
- **Lighter page:** three.js loads after the art is drawn, so the piece never waits for the game's library. Pressing `G` or KATAMAKI before it has arrived marks the button as loading, and the game opens as soon as it's ready. Space Mono comes from Google Fonts instead of being embedded (the mint build still embeds it), and only its regular weight is used.
- **Find your token without the seed:** the seed field also takes a token number (`51` or `#51`), an Aleatory or objkt link, or a wallet address or `.tez` name, which lists the wallet's pieces with thumbnails. A button with the wallet's name (.tez names in full, addresses shortened in the middle) then sits before KATAMAKI to reopen the list, for this tab; Random Seed or entering anything else removes it. Lookups use TzKT. `?token=51` in the address opens that token.
- **Share a wallet:** `?wallet=name.tez` (or an address) in a link looks the wallet up and shows its button; on its own it opens the wallet's first piece and its list, and with `&token=` or `&seed=` it keeps that piece. While a wallet is active the address carries `?wallet=` too, so copying it shares the wallet.
- **Links: token first.** A link with both `?token=` and `?seed=` (as the page writes them) shows the token: if the number was changed by hand and no longer matches the seed, the token's art is shown and the seed in the address bar corrected. A link with only a seed is drawn from it. The wallet button comes back on a reload only while the address bar still has `?wallet=`; open the page without it and no wallet is kept.
- The SEED pill, the home-screen icon, the background fill and the art at the bottom are as in the player.

### Katamaki on this page

Only in `v2/index.html` for now: the v2 edition (`makimono.html`, `makimono.mint.html`) and the root player are unchanged.

**Things to roll up**
- **A belt of plates:** a ring of conveyor-belt sushi plates. The plates and the sushi on them can be rolled up, and so can the belt's slats once you're big enough. The belt runs by the Direction trait: Forward the way the maki faces at the start, Reverse the other way (Text pieces are always Reverse). Before, it ran the same way on every piece.
- **An anthill:** a layered anthill some way off to the right of the start. Once you're big enough, the hill itself rolls up. On the roll it shrinks to at most 1.2× the roll's size, so a big hill (an ANTS planet's is 15 to 20) no longer hides the maki.
- **The piece's own art as a plaza:** a 13×13 plaza of raised tiles just ahead of the start, laid out like the art. Only the maki's tiles count. The background tiles are left out, and the eyes stay right in every pose. The roll can hop up onto the tiles.
- **Sky letters:** the message's letters in the sky can be rolled up too once the roll reaches them.
- **Real contact:** things are only picked up when the roll actually touches them. When the roll was big, it used to grab things from a distance.
- **Where pickups stick:** pickups stay where they touched along the roll, with a little random scatter, and stay off the filling ends. They used to be squeezed toward the middle.
- **Pieces sit on the roll.** Pickups still land as messily as before (any spin, a lean of up to 25°, some buried deep, most snug, some proud), but each is then checked against the roll under it: if more than half of its underside is clear of the roll, it is pressed in until half of it touches. Wide, tilted or perched pieces no longer hover beside the maki, and nothing is shrunk to do it (only the anthill, as before).
- **Long and wide things need a bigger roll.** A piece now also needs the roll's size to be at least 0.4 of its longest side (the roll at least 0.8 of it across), as well as half its size, so big fish, big shrimp and other long pieces can't be rolled up too soon.

**Growth and medals**
- **Growth is by area,** so each pickup adds size like real stuff stuck to a ball, and the last stretch is the slowest.
- **Goals:** Fast 2.7×, Normal 3.2×, Slow 3.7×, so every speed asks for the same growth per second. Speed sets the pace, not the difficulty.
- **Medals:** silver at 1.2× the goal and gold at 1.4×. They used to be 1.3× and 1.7×.
- **Big pieces count:** a plaza tile adds 0.035, a belt slat 0.05 and a sky letter 0.12.

**HUD and reports**
- **PLANET CLEARED:** how much of the planet you've rolled up, weighted by size. It rounds down, so it only says 100% when nothing is left. Near the end it reads "99% · N LEFT".
- **Last pickup:** a line under the scoreboard names the last thing you rolled up. A belt sushi rolled up with its plate reads SALMON + PLATE.
- **Stats:** the end screen, and the pause screen in free roll, list what made you grow, the ants you rolled up and when you passed each medal.
- **Gold titles:** a gold run with 95% or more of the planet cleared reads "NOTHING LEFT TO ROLL.", and one with 60% or more reads "THE PLANET IS GETTING NERVOUS." Below that, gold picks from its other lines. The losing line "The ants are nervous." is now "The ants are taking notes.", so "nervous" only appears once.

**Free roll**
- **Chopsticks:** two on every piece, in spots that change with the seed. They can be rolled up in free roll once the roll is at least the goal size and 1.05× theirs, and they wobble when you're close but not quite big enough.
- **Beacons:** once 10 or fewer things are left, each has a rising light column that follows it, so the last pieces are easy to find. Once on, they stay on for the rest of the round, and the timer slot (FREE ROLL) shows how many are left, e.g. 7 LEFT, then ALL CLEAR.
- **The last pieces get easier:** if nothing left is small enough to roll up and the roll hasn't grown for 4 seconds, the smallest piece left that counts toward clearing slowly shrinks, over about 3 seconds, to just under your size, with a little wobble, so a free roll never ends stuck (this mattered most in YOLO, where the last pieces can be giant). Rolling something up resets the wait.

**100% and OMAKASE**
- **Confetti:** clearing the whole planet sets off confetti in the piece's colours. It lands on the ground or on the maki, rests a few seconds and fades away.
- **OMAKASE:** after the confetti comes a new top grade, OMAKASE: "THE CHEF HAS LEFT THE BUILDING."
- **After OMAKASE:** Keep rolling still works, and the sky's marquee then scrolls NO MORE ANTS.
- **Back to art:** every end screen has a Back to art button.

**The chopsticks monster (after OMAKASE)**
- **When it comes:** after OMAKASE, if both chopsticks have been rolled up (before or after the OMAKASE screen), Keep rolling, let the confetti settle and the NO MORE ANTS marquee cross once (it always finishes its pass, even when the last chopsticks are rolled up mid-scroll). Then a giant chopsticks monster lands on the far side of the planet with a thump. The timer slot reads !!! and UH OH... scrolls in once the sky is clear (the pause screen's free-roll line reads !!! too)
- **The chase:** it hop-walks toward the roll, always turning to keep its eyes on it, and sends little chopsticks after it. They start few and slow (2 at a time, slower than the roll) and build up over about 45 s, to 10 at a time, faster than the roll and from every side. They hold their ground when they hit, and they bump only when they really touch the roll (the A of their legs against its side, not a whole roll and chopstick apart), and each bump knocks the roll a short way (about half to one roll width) away from the chopstick and more than half toward the giant, so sooner or later it runs into the giant. They can't be rolled up and nothing falls off.
- **The grab:** touching the giant sets it off. It closes on the maki and everything on it is knocked off and scattered around. Then it lifts the bare maki away out of the sky.
- **The restore:** the scattered pickups, and everything else that was moved or rolled up, fly back to where they stood at the start of the round. Belt slats and plates go back into their own spots on the stopped belt, and the rebuilt belt starts from where it stopped, so nothing jumps at the hand-over. The glitch waits until everything has landed. A glitch transition (blocky pixels with torn bands in the planet's own ground colours) hands over to the planet running again as it was at the start (ants walking, belt turning), without the maki. The planet turns slowly and the arrow keys, WASD or the stick spin it. NO MORE MAKI scrolls across the sky.
- **Shadows:** the giant, the little chopsticks and the scattered pickups cast a soft shadow on the ground that shrinks as they rise.
- **The pause screen:** from the monster's arrival on, it keeps the round's final numbers, taken when the monster arrives (so chopsticks rolled up after the OMAKASE screen count). On the final planet it becomes a closing card: a pink neon CLOSED sign, YOU ARE OFF THE MENU., the final numbers and Keep rolling.

**End, pause and restart screens**
- **Buttons:** stacked in one column on every screen, all as wide as the widest. Labels are centred; with key hints shown (keyboard), the label sits left and the key on the right. On short screens (landscape phones, 520px tall or less) they sit side by side in one row so everything fits.
- **Stats:** one row when there's room. On narrow touch screens (under 600px wide), a 2×2 grid; a fifth stat (chopsticks) gets its own centred row.
- **Short screens (landscape phones):** only the spacing tightens and the message gets wider, so everything fits without shrinking. If a very small screen still can't fit it all, it scrolls.
- **Turning the phone:** in a home-screen web app on iOS, turning the phone mid-game could leave taps landing away from the buttons (jump read as the stick, pause-screen taps missed). The page under the game no longer scrolls while it's open, and on every turn, resize or return to the app it's put back in place and the screen size re-read. A stick or hop held through a turn is let go. While the game is open, the page behind it takes the planet's sky colour, so turning the phone no longer flashes white.

**Fixes**
- **W A S D:** a letter key let go while Shift or Caps Lock had changed could stay held, so the roll kept reversing or turning. Letter keys now go by their place on the keyboard (any layout, Shift or Caps Lock), and every key is let go when the window loses focus.
- **Beacons:** the planet's faint atmosphere no longer cuts off the tops of the beams, and a beam no longer swings off in a wrong direction for a moment when its thing is rolled up.
- **Home-screen icon:** drawn on the piece's background colour, so it's never black or see-through where the art leaves pixels empty.
- **Free roll pause line:** in caps, small and dim, it shows only where the round stands, as the timer slot does: FREE ROLL, 3 LEFT, ALL CLEAR, then !!! in the chase and FINAL on the final planet.
- **Knocked-off pickups:** things knocked off the roll now cast a shadow on the ground while they fly, and keep one once they land.
- **Stuck by the belt:** the belt could carry the roll into something too big to roll up beside it (a big oshibori, say) and pin it there, unable to roll or hop away. Now the roll slips on the belt instead of being pushed into it.
- **Zoom:** bring the camera closer to the maki. On desktop, scroll or pinch the trackpad over the game, or press `Z` to switch between close and the usual view. On touch, a magnifier button next to pause switches, and a two-finger pinch zooms (the stick lets go while two fingers are down). Close up, the camera is at 45% of its usual distance and looks down a little less, so you see more of the maki's side; it eases there over about a quarter of a second. The zoom stays for the rest of the visit, and stays close past gold instead of pulling back to the whole planet. The ending keeps its own camera. On the final planet the same controls zoom like Google Earth: from the whole planet down to just above the ground, the view tipping forward toward the horizon and the turning slowing as you get close; it opens zoomed out each time. `Z` is on the shortcuts card.
- **Photos:** press `C` (or the camera button on touch, next to zoom and pause) to take a photo of the world as it is on screen, without the HUD, buttons or their text. It is laid out like the art's info panel: the shot fills a 1040 square (the GIF's size), with the panel's thin 1000 square frame inside it 20 in from the edge, a solid bottom band in place of the traits, and PROJECT MAKIMONO and the logo in their usual spots, all in the piece's own rice colour. Type your own caption on the band, in the nori colour at the size of a trait value. It wraps onto a second line when it runs long (wrapped prettily, so no word is left alone on the last line), and only shrinks if two lines still won't hold it; `Enter` starts the second line yourself. It never goes past two lines. It stays for the next photo until you save or share one, then the field starts empty. The photo develops from dark grey over about two seconds. Save downloads a 1040 PNG (2080 on large screens); Share opens the share sheet where the device can share files. A round you're playing pauses while the photo is up, and Back (`Esc`) carries on. `C` is on the shortcuts card.
- **Lighter on slow phones:** typing a message redraws the art 150 ms after the last key instead of on every key, only the last 6 drawn versions of the art are kept in memory (typing used to keep one per key), and the game's hint timer no longer runs while the game is closed.
- **A secret magnet** for playing through quickly: while the game is on, `M` pulls in everything the roll can already pick up, from a range that grows with the roll (as Chase's magnet did), and `Shift`+`M` is SUPER, which pulls from anywhere on the planet. Belt sushi flies in on its plate; once the first belt slat or plaza tile has been rolled up, the rest fly in too; the anthill and the sky letters come once the roll is big enough. While it's on, a small yellow line at the top says MAGNET MODE or SUPER MAGNET MODE. It isn't listed on the shortcuts card.
- **Chopsticks on the restored planet:** they no longer twitch back and forth. The hidden maki still sat where the round ended, and chopsticks kept turning to face that spot and circling it, flipping round every frame when they walked over it. On the restored planet they now face the way they walk and turn smoothly.
- **The plaza keeps one layout:** it used to turn the art to face the camera about 1.5 s into the round, and the restored planet picked its own way again, sometimes mirrored (text read backwards). It now always lies the same fixed way, in the round and on the restored planet, and can't be mirrored.
- **Knocked-off pieces can always be rolled back up:** anything a chopstick or ant knocks off your roll can be rolled up again at any size, and it no longer blocks you or wobbles as if it were too big. Before, a piece needed your size from when you first took it, so a cloud rolled up at just the right moment, then knocked off (which shrinks the maki a little), could end up stuck on the ground, most noticeably in free roll where there is little left to grow on.
- **Pieces sit on the roll:** a coin no longer perches above the roll (the giant coin is wedged in by a third of its height), and anything knocked off and rolled up again sits on the maki like a fresh pickup instead of hovering just off it (a tilted or long piece used to hover the most, and big ones barely sank in).
- **Update notice:** when a newer version of the game is on the site, a toast along the top says THE CHEF UPDATED THE MENU with REFRESH. The page checks a small `version.json` next to it when it loads, when you come back to the tab, and every 30 minutes; the toast stays on the art and on the game's start, pause and end screens until you refresh, and hides while a round is played. REFRESH reloads the page with the new version in the address (so no cache serves the old copy) and, from the game, opens the game again. Opened from a file or offline, it stays quiet. Only the game is versioned, as the art never changes; `version.json` has to be copied along with `index.html`.
- **No slow motion on slow computers:** below 20 fps the game used to run slower than real time (each frame moved it on by at most 0.05 s). Now a slow frame runs a few short steps and draws only the last, so the game keeps real speed; it is choppier, but nothing is skipped through.
- **Quality steps down where it helps:** on screens at 1.5× sharpness or less (most PC monitors, like a 1080p laptop) the first quality level changed nothing, so the check saw no gain and locked quality at full. It now skips that level and goes straight to 1× sharpness with half the decor.
- **Starting quality from the graphics chip:** drawing without graphics acceleration starts at the lowest level, and older Intel graphics (most 2019 laptops) start a level or two down. Other chips start at full as before.
- **The welcome screen** draws at the game's quality level instead of always at full.
- **A lighter giant-chopsticks ending at low quality:** at the two lowest levels, only the first 60 things that moved fly home in the restore (the rest are simply back in place), and at most 40 falling pickups cast a shadow at once.
- **Reduced motion:** with the device's Reduce Motion setting on, there are no camera or planet shakes, no glitch in the mosaic hand-over, a third of the confetti, and the sky letters cross slower and barely bob.

**Paul mode (Text PAUL HBD)**
- **When:** art with the Text PAUL HBD pattern plays in Paul mode. It takes priority over Tezos mode; the other modes can combine with it.
- **The ground:** about 4 in 10 ingredients are swapped for birthday things: mostly red glasses (thick, near-square frames), plus birthday cakes (two tiers, three lit candles), gifts, party hats and ghosts. Half the ghosts start wearing glasses, and about 4 in 10 start in a party hat.
- **The belts:** about 4 in 10 plates carry glasses, a cake, a gift or a party hat instead of food. Ghosts never ride the belts.
- **Gifts:** 6 in 10 are wrapped in a colour from the art's own palette, the rest in red. The ribbon is yellow, or red on a light box so it still shows.
- **Ghosts:** see-through sheets with Hype eyes that float up and down. They walk anywhere, over water and sometimes under the ground, popping back up when they stop. They can't be rolled up and don't count toward clearing. When they walk into ants or chopsticks they shove them aside.
- **How many ghosts:** at least 13 on every Paul mode planet (more if they land there by chance), and at least one of them taller than the maki is wide at the start of the round. These extras are picked from their own random numbers, so the rest of the planet comes out the same.
- **Glasses, hats and ghosts:** a ghost puts on any glasses or party hat it walks into, if it isn't wearing one already. Only a ghost that started out with something goes looking for it: one that started in glasses heads for the nearest pair on the ground once its own are snatched, and one that started in a hat heads for the nearest hat once its own is knocked off (including ones knocked off the roll). Ghosts that started without either never go looking. The hat sits on top of the head, tipped to one side. Anything a ghost takes comes off the round's total.
- **Rolling into a ghost's face:** its glasses go on the roll (they never count toward clearing), and its party hat is bumped off and thrown clear, like a piece knocked off the roll; bigger ghosts throw it further, and a ghost can't put its own hat back on for 3 s, so it doesn't catch it again as it lands. The hat lands on the ground, where you can roll it up or a ghost can put it back on; it doesn't count as lost to hits. The end card shows **GLASSES SNATCHED** when you took any.
- **Lighter ghosts:** a ghost looks for something to pick up about 7 times a second instead of every frame.

**Tezos mode (Text TEZOS)**
- **When:** art whose Message or Pattern contains "tezos" (and isn't Paul mode).
- **Tezos coins:** about 1 in 4 ingredients, on the ground and on the belts, is swapped for a Tezos coin: a chunky 12-sided toy coin in Tezos blue, with a raised plateau on each face and the real ꜩ logo (traced from the Tezos mark) raised in white on both sides. Each coin is two meshes, so it costs about the same to draw as a piece of food.
- **One giant coin:** every Tezos planet also has a single coin 6.5 to 8 times the size, placed from its own random numbers so the rest of the planet comes out the same.
- **Coin trails:** every Tezos planet also has 5 short straight lines of 5 to 7 coins to follow, like coin trails in a platformer. They are placed from their own random numbers, so the rest of the planet comes out the same, and they count toward the coin total.
- **Coin counter:** from the first coin you roll up, the OBJECTS counter in the top left turns into a coin counter: a little coin (drawn like the 3D one) and "× 012/050", the coins rolled up so far out of every coin that has turned up this round. Each coin counts once, even if a hit knocks it off and you roll it up again, and coins the belts bring in add to the total. The switch slides: the old line slides up and fades, then the new one slides down into place, so it never moves over the GOT line below.
- **Last 10 coins shine:** once 10 or fewer coins are left out on the planet, they spin like a game coin, glint, give a little hop now and then, and three white sparkles twinkle round each, so they're easier to find. The sparkles keep the same size on screen however far away the coin is, so they still show on a phone. With the device's Reduce Motion setting on, the coins don't hop.
- **All coins collected:** when the last coin is in, the counter stays still while the coin icon flips once in 3D (its thick edge showing as it turns), white sparkles twinkle round it, the digits flash white and a white shine sweeps across them. A moment later the counter slides back to OBJECTS for the rest of the round. The end card shows TEZOS COINS, for example 050/050. With the device's Reduce Motion setting on, the flip and sparkles are left out and the slides become fades.
- **Baking rewards:** a nod to Tezos staking, which is called baking. Every 12 seconds, right up until the planet is restored (free roll included), each coin on your roll earns 0.05%, shown on the GOT line as BAKING REWARDS, for example +1.00% for 20 coins. The number follows only the coins you hold, so it never drops as you grow; the maki grows by that share of its starting size, so it matches the real growth early on and is more of a fun number later. The pause and end cards add the round's rewards up under TEZOS COINS. Coins knocked off stop earning until you roll them up again.

**Ants mode (Text ANTS)**
- On top of the ant planet above (the 21 extra small ants): **3 giant ants**, about twice the size of the biggest normal ants, and **ants on the belts**, where about 3 in 10 plates carry a small ant instead of food.
- **Radioactive ants:** the 3 giant ants and about 1 in 6 of the others are radioactive. Their body glows a dim toxic green and slowly breathes, each at its own pace, with a soft green glow on the ground under them; the giant ones also give off a few tiny specks that drift up and fade. Rolled up, one shows GOT RADIOACTIVE ANT and still counts as an ant. Which ants are picked doesn't change the planet's layout.

**YOLO mode (Text YOLO)**
- **When:** art whose Message or Pattern contains "yolo".
- **Giant food and scenery, low gravity:** about half the ingredients on the ground, and about half the trees, bushes, rocks, flowers and mushrooms, grow by one size picked for the planet (2.5 to 4 times), and gravity drops to 0.25 to 0.45 of normal. Both are picked at random each time the planet is built and are always strong, so no YOLO planet looks ordinary. The planet is laid out the same; only the sizes change.
- **Floating food:** loose food on the ground slowly floats up, about 1 to 4 units (higher for bigger pieces and lighter gravity), and bobs there. Its shadow stays on the ground and shrinks the higher it goes. Food on plates, belts and tiles, pieces knocked off the roll and ghosts stay down. A floating piece is as easy to roll up: reach is checked where it would sit on the ground, and it drops back as it sticks.
- **Floaty hops:** your hops go higher and stay up much longer (up to about 3.7 times as long at 0.25 gravity), and the fall takes longer than the rise, so the maki eases off the top and drifts down instead of dropping. Knock-back hops keep the normal arc.

**Planet conditions (every pattern but Text: Scroll, Diagonal, Ripple, Sparkle, Chase, Squiggle, Wave, Short Wave, Checker, Maki)**
- **When:** art whose Pattern is Scroll, Diagonal, Ripple, Sparkle, Chase, Squiggle, Wave, Short Wave, Checker or Maki. The planet gets one condition of its own, taken from how that pattern moves in the art. The terrain is the same as before; only how things move on it changes. The restored planet has no condition.
- **LANDSLIDE (Scroll):** the ground is split into 8 bands round a tilted axis; the 6 middle bands slide in alternating directions, each at its own speed (the two at the poles stay still), and carry you and everything loose on them along. Faint slats on the ground show the flow, clear for the first 3 seconds, then fading out; they come back briefly when you cross into a new band.
- **WINDY (Diagonal):** a steady wind with gusts pushes you and loose things across the planet (big things hardly move). Trees nearby lean with it, clouds drift, and faint streaks show the wind at the start and when a gust picks up.
- **TREMORS (Ripple):** every 4 to 6 seconds a faint spot warns where a shock will start, then a thin ring spreads out from it. Everything it passes reacts once, just for a moment: trees, bushes, flowers and mushrooms sway away from the shock and spring back, rocks and pickups hop, ants hop and scatter, chopsticks hop and wobble (only how they look: what blocks you and what you can roll up stay the same); when it reaches you, you get bumped and pushed and the camera shakes (no shake with Reduce Motion).
- **METEOR SHOWER (Sparkle):** every 5 to 7.5 seconds a burst of 5 to 8 meteors comes down near you over 1.6 seconds, all slanting in from the same side of the sky, with now and then a lone one in between; about a third land just ahead of you. Each glints on the ground for a second first, then the near-black meteor streaks down with a white trail and hits with a quick ring that everything within 5 reacts to, as with a tremor; if you are close you get bumped, and the camera shakes a little. On land it leaves a dark scorch mark that fades over 12 seconds and 3 or 4 small heaps of flat, near-black crumbs that burst out of the impact and arc to where they land. Each heap can be rolled up (worth about one normal piece for the lot) but doesn't count toward clearing the planet. At most 30 lie about: past that, the oldest shrink away, so every meteor still leaves crumbs.
- **STAMPEDE (Chase):** food you are big enough to roll up spots you from 9 away (further as you grow) and runs, zigzagging and hopping, a bit slower than you; when you are nearly on it, it jinks to one side. Each piece runs for 2.6 seconds, then stops for a short breather. It won't run into the sea, and scenery stops it, so you can corner it.
- **RAINBOW TRAIL (Squiggle):** after Chromie Squiggle, the generative art the pattern is named for. The maki draws a flat rainbow line on the ground as it rolls, wider as the maki grows, with round ends and smooth bends; the line lasts 30 seconds, and its tail shortens smoothly. Where it crosses itself, the newer part lies on top. Cross your own line (any part more than 2.5 seconds old) to close a loop: a wave of colour runs through the whole line and magnet mode comes on for 5 seconds (a magnet you turned on yourself is left alone). Each crossing is its own loop, so a figure 8 makes two, and each new loop starts the 5 seconds again. Turning on the spot, very small circles, hops and being knocked about by a chopstick, an ant or a bump don't count.
- **TIDE (Wave):** the sea slowly rises and falls, once every 50 seconds, up to 1.1 above its usual line, covering low ground and leaving it again; the round starts at low tide. In the water, loose food floats up, bobs on the waves and drifts with a current round the planet, and stays where it ends up when the water goes; it stops at the water's edge and against scenery, and can still be rolled up while it floats. The maki is slowed the deeper it is, by up to half in water as deep as it is tall (a big maki wades through), and leaves a splash behind it.
- **RIPTIDE (Short Wave):** the same water, quicker (every 18 seconds) and lower, with rougher waves that rock you from side to side and a faster current.
- **GRAND PRIX (Checker):** a race gate, two posts with a checkered banner, stands up a little way ahead of you, just in front of food you can roll up, with the food nearest it hopping into a line behind it (up to 4 pieces). Roll through it, either way, for a speed boost that carries you down the line, and the next gate stands up ahead, like a trail of rings. The boost is 50% faster for 3 seconds; reach the next gate within 10 seconds and each one adds 15% and 0.5 seconds, up to twice as fast for 5 seconds, and it grows with the maki so a big roll feels it as much. It works backing up too, and white dust kicks up behind you. Each gate is a CHECKERED FLAG on the GOT line, with the run counted (CHECKERED FLAG x3). Gates stand further off the faster you go and the wider you turn, so there is room to turn onto them, and the way you are heading counts, backing up included. A gate you miss goes after 30 seconds, or once you are well past it (never in its first 4 seconds), and a new one stands up. Late in the round, when little loose food is left, gates stand in front of whatever is still left to roll up: scenery, plaza tiles or belt slates. The banner's checks are near-black and a soft white.
  - **Finding the next gate:** the camera never turns with you, so a gate ahead can be off screen (most often on a phone in portrait). While it is, a small checkered barricade with a triangle beside it sits at the screen edge and points the way; it fades once the gate is in view. Spots already on screen get a slight preference, and the first gate waits 1 s for the camera to settle.
- **FUTOMAKI (Maki):** after the maki that started the collection, the roll becomes a long log, rolling side on. It starts as a normal roll and grows mostly longer, not fatter: its radius grows with the 0.7th power of its size and its length with the 1.8th. It picks up along its whole length, with a little more reach toward the ends, and everything sticks where it touched, confetti and the giant chopsticks included. Its ends stop against scenery you are too small for and shove big food along, it turns 45% slower. What it picks up is what its round side really touches, measured along the ground, so nothing on the far side of the planet is ever in reach: low, flat things (food, belt slates, plaza tiles) only once the log is nearly over them, tall things (trees, rocks) a little further out, and towards its ends a little less, as it rises off the curving ground. Belt slates go up to eight at a time, so a full-length log rolling along the belt takes them all, and sushi riding the belt is rolled up with its plate, along the whole log. The anthill counts only its real base, so it is taken when the log is over it. When the little chopsticks knock it during the chase, it slides back along the ground like a normal roll. Since the log sweeps up so much more, each piece adds half its usual size, so it reaches gold about as late as a normal roll. Nothing starts stuck to it: food and ants under the log at the start are moved clear, and scenery, tiles and slates under it are left alone until it has rolled off them.
- **The condition label:** top right, under the timer, an icon and a name (LANDSLIDE, WINDY, TREMORS, METEOR SHOWER, STAMPEDE, RAINBOW TRAIL, TIDE, RIPTIDE, GRAND PRIX, FUTOMAKI) at 75% white, shown only during a round. Text modes get one too: COIN RUSH (Tezos), PARTY TIME (Paul), ANT APOCALYPSE (Ants), THIS IS FINE (YOLO); with both, the text mode comes first. The icons are from Phosphor (regular weight, MIT). On touch the label also sits right under the timer, and the pause, zoom and camera buttons slide down below it while it shows (and back up when it goes), so they never cover it; if touch switches on mid-round, at the first tap, the label moves at once.
- All marks and cues are kept subtle, never bright white. If anything in a condition goes wrong, it switches off for that round and the game carries on.
- **On slower devices** the conditions follow the quality levels (desktop is unchanged). From High down, only pickups within 40 of you drift in the wind or on a sliding band; at Medium each moves every other frame and at Low every third, covering the same distance. The tremor ring is drawn with fewer segments (72, 48, 36, 24 from Ultra to Low). From Medium the landslide slats move every other frame, and at Low one in three is left out. At Medium only things within 40 of you react to a tremor; at Low only pickups hop. From Medium a meteor burst has 3 or 4 meteors, the rainbow trail keeps up to 500 points instead of 900, the tide water is drawn with fewer faces and a GRAND PRIX line has up to 3 pieces instead of 4. In TIDE and RIPTIDE, from High down food further than 40 from you works out its float height every 8/60 of a second, and from Medium nearby food every 1/30 of a second, keeping its last height in between. These are timed in seconds rather than frames, and ants fleeing a tremor turn by time too, so a 120 Hz screen plays and costs the same as a 60 Hz one. The push and bump on you are the same at every level. On every device, FUTOMAKI only checks things within reach of the log (it used to go over the whole planet twice a frame), and a GRAND PRIX gate that cannot be placed waits a quarter second before trying again instead of trying every frame.

**Rule changes**
- **Chase:** the magnet and its 9 chopsticks are off. Chase plays like the other patterns, with 2 chopsticks.
- **Chopstick counts:** every pattern gets 2 chopsticks. Before, Sparkle, Checker and Ripple had 2 and the rest had 1.
